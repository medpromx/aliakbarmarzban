Port reform debates often start with the label: should the port become a **landlord port**? The more useful starting point is accountability.

## Beyond the label

Widely used reference frameworks describe four models — service, tool, landlord and fully private — based on who owns infrastructure, who owns superstructure and who employs the workforce. In practice, most ports are hybrids.

What matters for performance is whether:

- regulatory and commercial roles are separated where they conflict;
- concession agreements contain measurable, enforceable obligations;
- the port authority has the capability to monitor operators;
- government retains the strategic decisions that belong to it.

## Where to begin

An independent governance diagnostic maps mandates, decision rights and performance data before reform options are designed. This avoids reforms that change the structure but not the results.
