Since 1 January 2024, under the mandatory amendments to the Annex to the Convention on Facilitation of International Maritime Traffic (FAL.14(46)), the relevant public authorities in the ports of Contracting Governments have been required to implement a maritime single window (MSW) for the electronic exchange of documents on the arrival, stay and departure of ships. Even so, many port authorities discover after buying expensive platforms that organisational resistance, overlapping administrative powers and inconsistent data stop the system from working in practice. This challenge stems from ignoring a structural distinction and from the lack of a governance framework.

## 1. Separating the maritime single window (MSW) from the port community system (PCS)

One fundamental misunderstanding is to confuse the maritime single window with port community systems:

- **Maritime single window (MSW):** an integrated, central national or port gateway for completing legal formalities between government and operators (B2G and G2B), which collects the information required by public agencies (customs, health, fisheries, immigration, coast guard and the maritime authority) according to the FAL forms;
- **Port community system (PCS):** a multi-purpose digital platform for optimising and exchanging logistics and commercial documents between private and public actors (B2B and B2G), which coordinates supply chain processes, cargo clearance, warehousing and container movements.

## 2. The pillars of data governance, process re-engineering and cyber security

Without administrative re-engineering, digitalisation only "puts the inefficiencies of old paperwork online". An effective MSW needs three institutional components:

- **Data ownership and quality:** naming the authorities responsible for the accuracy of each data field, aligning with the shared vocabulary of the IMO Compendium and removing data redundancy;
- **Inter-agency data exchange agreements:** clear rules on access, privacy and legal powers so that parallel paper forms are not demanded on the quay;
- **Cyber security governance:** audit mechanisms and cyber risk controls against malware attacks and theft of cargo document data.

## Three strategic questions for senior managers of port digital transformation

- **Organisational ownership of the action:** the director-general for information and communication technology together with the deputy directorate for port and maritime affairs.

1. **Before software development, have legal bottlenecks and traditional inter-agency processes been redesigned as a whole?**
2. **Has the system's data model been calibrated against the international standards of the IMO FAL Compendium and the WCO Data Model?**
3. **What governance structure has been set up for security accountability and for continuity of operations during cyber disruption?**

## Management conclusion

A maritime single window is not a project to buy server equipment; it is a comprehensive plan to change how ports are administered. Technology only provides the platform for change; what ensures smooth ship movements and lower port costs is institutional discipline and coordination between agencies in managing data.
