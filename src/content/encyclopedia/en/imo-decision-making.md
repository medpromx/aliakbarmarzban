## Separating the policy-making body from the technical forum
In IMO's institutional architecture, the Assembly and the Council have different roles from the specialist committees. The Maritime Safety Committee (**MSC**), the Marine Environment Protection Committee (**MEPC**), the Facilitation Committee (**FAL**) and the Legal Committee (**LEG**) act within their own mandates. Sub-committees such as **III** and **HTW** help develop technical detail and examine specialist subjects; but the presence of a subject on an agenda does not mean a new requirement has been adopted.

## From agenda to legal effect
A subject can start with a proposal from a government or a competent body, then be examined in a committee or sub-committee and reach the adoption stage as the text of an amendment, a guideline or a decision. For a treaty amendment, the process of acceptance and entry into force, including the tacit acceptance procedure in the relevant instruments, is decisive. Correct analysis therefore needs three separate dates: adoption of the text, its acceptance and the start of implementation.

## Why meeting reports are not enough
A meeting report is essential for regulatory intelligence, but it does not take the place of the final official text. When analysing meeting outcomes, describe the status of each item precisely with words such as "considered", "approved", "adopted" or "referred to the next session". Then identify the internal stakeholders, the financial effects and the person responsible for follow-up.

## The management product
For each important subject, build a decision card with six fields: document reference; stage in the process; likely consequence; degree of certainty; responsible unit; next review date. In this way the manager receives a set of decisions that can be tracked instead of a flood of reports.
