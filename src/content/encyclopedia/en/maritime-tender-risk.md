## A good tender starts with defining the problem
Ambiguity in the scope of services, the evaluation criteria or the point of delivery turns into change requests, delay and disputes during performance. The **RFP** should define the employer's functional need in measurable terms, not just list equipment and repeat documents. Qualification criteria, technical and financial evaluation and contract conditions must be kept separate from the start.

## Six knots in risk allocation
In maritime projects, change in law, restrictions on port access, weather conditions, baseline technical data, force majeure and insurance liability are sensitive points. For each risk, ask which party is able to prevent, reduce or insure it. Transferring a risk by contract to a party that has no control over it can raise the bid price or lead to multiple claims during performance.

## Transparent evaluation and conflicts of interest
The evaluation committee must follow the published criteria, weightings and documentation of its decisions. Changing criteria after bids are received, or subjective evaluation, weakens the credibility and defensibility of the process. A conflict of interest declaration form and minutes of the decision are essential for institutional control.

## The practical output: two complementary documents
For every tender, prepare an "RFP control checklist" and a "risk allocation matrix". The matrix should include a description of each risk, its likelihood and impact, the risk owner, the contract clause and the monitoring method. In contracts based on BIMCO forms, the text must also be reviewed by a specialist to fit the subject, the governing law and the terms of the transaction.
