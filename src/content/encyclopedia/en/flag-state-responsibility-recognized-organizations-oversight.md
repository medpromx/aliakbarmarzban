In contemporary maritime administration it is common for maritime authorities to rely on classification societies to perform technical tasks. However, relying on these bodies alone, without an independent umbrella of oversight, exposes the flag to serious legal and governance vulnerability.

## 1. The legal line between "delegating a technical task" and "ultimate sovereign responsibility"

Under Article 94 of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) and Part 2 of the Code for the Implementation of IMO Instruments (III Code), exercising effective jurisdiction and control over ships in technical, administrative and social matters is the exclusive duty of the flag State. International treaties, including SOLAS regulation XI-1/1, the MARPOL Annexes and Regulation 5.1.1 of the Maritime Labour Convention (MLC 2006), allow States to delegate surveys and the issue of statutory documents to "recognised organisations" (ROs). Nevertheless, these instruments make clear that delegating executive tasks does not mean delegating responsibility; the flag State always remains ultimately accountable for the safety and compliance of its fleet.

## 2. The pillars of institutional oversight under the RO Code

Under the Code for Recognized Organizations (RO Code, adopted by Resolutions MSC.349(92) and MEPC.237(65)) and paragraphs 18 to 20 of the III Code, delegation requires an active governance framework:

- **A written delegation agreement:** a formal contract that sets out precisely the scope of authority, the reporting requirements and the national technical instructions.
- **A documented oversight programme:** unannounced supplementary inspections on board ships and periodic audits of the RO's local offices.
- **Data-driven performance monitoring:** continuous tracking of the fleet's detention rate in port State control (PSC) inspections and identification of deficiencies attributable to the classification society's work.

## Three practical management questions for maritime leaders

Before accepting the certificates issued, senior maritime administration managers should answer three key questions:

1. **Does the formal agreement with each RO cover clear limits of authority, requirements for immediate reporting of deficiencies and compliance with national interpretations?**
2. **Does the maritime administration have the specialist staff and resources needed for supplementary inspections and direct audits of RO performance?**
3. **What mechanism has been established to analyse the ROs' performance record in annual PSC reports and to apply corrective actions?**

## Closing note and implementation outlook

Delegating authority to ROs is a tool for compensating for administrative limits, not a way of escaping responsibility. In IMO Member State Audit Scheme (IMSAS) assessments, any failure of a recognised organisation is recorded directly as a failure of the flag State. Real effectiveness is assured only when technical competence is linked to active sovereign oversight.
