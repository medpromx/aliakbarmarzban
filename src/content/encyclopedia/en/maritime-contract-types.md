## Similar instruments, different effects
A **charterparty**, a service contract, a port concession agreement and an **MoU** may look as if they have similar chapters, but their purpose, duration, parties, risks and legal effects differ. In particular, the title "memorandum of understanding" does not on its own decide whether a text is binding or non-binding; the nature of the obligations and the governing law must also be examined.

## From need to legal structure
If the aim is to buy a deliverable service, the acceptance criteria, payment, guarantees and liability for delay must be clear. In a long-term port concession, property rights, investment, tariffs, monitoring and termination matter more. In institutional cooperation, a non-binding MoU may be suitable for declaring an area of cooperation, but confidentiality and intellectual property clauses can create independent obligations.

## The core risk-control clauses
Scope of work, change of scope, baseline information, force majeure, insurance, limitation of liability, governing law and dispute resolution must be consistent with the commercial and operational logic of the project. A precise text is not effective enough without naming who in the organisation is responsible for implementing each clause. It is suggested that a "contract owner" and warning points be set for each sensitive clause.

## Screening before signature
Prepare a one-page note covering the aims of the transaction, the legal options, the signatory's authority, the main risks and the issues that need a specialist lawyer's opinion. Governance advice can help with the decision and risk structure, but it does not replace case-specific legal review.
