## Why news monitoring is not enough
Following maritime regulation is not just collecting news from international meetings; the organisation must identify which developments affect the fleet, the port, certificates, contracts and the budget. "Regulatory intelligence" means turning many signals into a short list of time-bound decisions, each with its source, degree of certainty and the person responsible for action.

## A four-layer architecture
The first layer is **primary sources**: the texts of conventions and resolutions and official information from IMO, the ILO and regional authorities. The second layer is **legal classification**: in force, adopted but future, under consideration or recommended guidance. The third layer is **exposure assessment**: which ship types, trade routes, training centres or organisational units are affected? The fourth layer is **management action**: naming an owner, a deadline, a budget and a completion measure.

## A one-page report for the board
An effective report should answer five questions: what has happened, what is the exact document, what effect does it have on us, what options are there and what decision is needed now? For example, a draft climate policy with high uncertainty needs scenario planning, not the declaration of a firm requirement in a contract. By contrast, a fixed implementation date must be linked to an operational compliance plan.

## Quality control
To stop errors reaching managers, keep the document version, the date of the last check, the reviewer's name and the primary source of each conclusion. It is best to set a monthly review meeting and an immediate alert mechanism for high-risk amendments. This frequency is a management suggestion and is not necessarily set in international instruments.
